### Slide 6 · Research
- **Competitive analysis:** The Overwhelmed Entertainment Seeker
- A long-term subscriber who enjoys watching content regularly but increasingly struggles to find something worth committing to.

Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:
- To quickly find a film or series they genuinely want to watch and feel that their subscription consistently delivers value.

Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:
- I open the app, scroll for like twenty minutes, and close it without watching anything." 

Despite access to thousands of titles, the user experiences choice overload, repetitive recommendations, ineffective discovery, and ultimately abandons the session without viewing anything. This creates the perception that the service is a "warehouse" of content rather than a trusted guide to great content.

Workaround summary - External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:
- When the user cannot confidently find something to watch, they increasingly rely on external sources to curate the decision for them.

 The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:
- Step 1: Open the streaming app
User intends to relax and watch something.
Begins browsing recommendations and home-page content.

- Step 2: Experience choice overload
Scrolls through rows of content.
Encounters repetitive recommendations and irrelevant suggestions.
Feels uncertain whether any option will be worth their time.

Research signals
"I open the app, scroll for like twenty minutes, and close it without watching anything."
"Discovering anything new feels like a chore."
Users describe the service as a "warehouse."

- Step 3: Leave the platform

The user opens alternative sources to make the decision for them:

External tools
- IMDb Top Lists
- Rotten Tomatoes
- Letterboxd
- Reddit recommendation threads
- TikTok movie recommendations
- YouTube reviewers
- Friend recommendations via text or social media
- Competitor recommendation emails containing curated selections

This behavior is directly reflected in the interviews:
- "I trust a recommendation from a friend more than the algorithm."
- "A competitor emails me two hand-picked films a week and I watch both."

Step 4: Build a shortlist externally
- User searches for "best movies this week."
- Reads reviews or watches recommendation videos.
- Creates a mental shortlist of 1-3 titles.

Step 5: Return to the streaming platform
- Searches for the specific title.
- Watches the chosen content if available.

If unavailable, may leave again for another service. Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”: It increases decision fatigue

The customer spends time evaluating recommendations across multiple channels before even beginning entertainment consumption.

The experience feels like work rather than leisure. 
It delays value realization
The user subscribes to enjoy content, but a growing portion of their session is spent searching rather than watching.
From the customer's perspective, the subscription delivers access to content but not confidence in what to watch.
- **Journey map:** Future-state journey:
Discover: Browse Spotlight rail → instantly see curated choices → reduces choice overload
Trust: Read curator rationale → gain confidence in recommendations → reduces recommendation skepticism
Decide: Select from trusted shortlist → choose faster → reduces decision paralysis
Enjoy & Return: Watch and follow curated paths → discover consistently great content → reduces churn risk

Variant (B):

- As an overwhelmed entertainment seeker, I want to see a Handpicked by Streamline Editors shortlist when I open StreamLine so that I can start choosing immediately.

Success Criteria 
- Median Time-to-Play improves by at least 5 minutes (30 → 25 min). p < 0.05. 
- Playback start Rate is not lower than control. 
- Early Abandonment increases by no more than 1 percentage point. 
- Sample size target is reached. 
- Data quality checks pass.

Shipping criteria
- We will SHIP if Median same-session time-to-play improves by ≥ 5 at p<0.05 and Early abandonment rate: percentage of successful playback starts where the selected title accumulates fewer than five minutes of active viewing before the user exits, excluding technical playback failures. does not reach Cannot increase more than 1% after 14 calendar days. 
- We will ITERATE if direction is positive but lift is below MDE. 
- We will KILL if the primary metric shows no improvement or moves negatively. The read date is fixed at the end of 14 calendar days, no results reviewed before then.
