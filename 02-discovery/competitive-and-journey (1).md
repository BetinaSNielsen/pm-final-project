# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** The Overwhelmed Entertainment Seeker
 A long-term subscriber who enjoys watching content regularly but increasingly struggles to find something worth committing to.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** To quickly find a film or series they genuinely want to watch and feel that their subscription consistently delivers value.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** I open the app, scroll for like twenty minutes, and close it without watching anything." Despite access to thousands of titles, the user experiences choice overload, repetitive recommendations, ineffective discovery, and ultimately abandons the session without viewing anything. This creates the perception that the service is a "warehouse" of content rather than a trusted guide to great content.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** When the user cannot confidently find something to watch, they increasingly rely on external sources to curate the decision for them.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Step 1: Open the streaming app
User intends to relax and watch something.
Begins browsing recommendations and home-page content.
Step 2: Experience choice overload
Scrolls through rows of content.
Encounters repetitive recommendations and irrelevant suggestions.
Feels uncertain whether any option will be worth their time.

Research signals

"I open the app, scroll for like twenty minutes, and close it without watching anything."
"Discovering anything new feels like a chore."
Users describe the service as a "warehouse."
Step 3: Leave the platform

The user opens alternative sources to make the decision for them:

External tools

IMDb Top Lists
Rotten Tomatoes
Letterboxd
Reddit recommendation threads
TikTok movie recommendations
YouTube reviewers
Friend recommendations via text or social media
Competitor recommendation emails containing curated selections

This behavior is directly reflected in the interviews:

"I trust a recommendation from a friend more than the algorithm."

"A competitor emails me two hand-picked films a week and I watch both."

Step 4: Build a shortlist externally
User searches for "best movies this week."
Reads reviews or watches recommendation videos.
Creates a mental shortlist of 1-3 titles.
Step 5: Return to the streaming platform
Searches for the specific title.
Watches the chosen content if available.
If unavailable, may leave again for another service.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** It increases decision fatigue

The customer spends time evaluating recommendations across multiple channels before even beginning entertainment consumption.

The experience feels like work rather than leisure.

It delays value realization

The user subscribes to enjoy content, but a growing portion of their session is spent searching rather than watching.

From the customer's perspective, the subscription delivers access to content but not confidence in what to watch.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** The customer is not actually searching for content. They are searching for confidence that the next two hours of their evening will be well spent. Today, they increasingly rely on friends, reviewers, social media, and competitors to create that confidence, which means the platform is losing ownership of one of the most important moments in the customer journey: deciding what to watch.
